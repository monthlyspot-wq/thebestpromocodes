What you give up compared with a licensed NJ site

A regulator-backed complaint process

The DGE requires licensed operators “to have effective systems in place for receiving, recording, managing and investigating Internet gaming customer complaints.” If a licensed casino can’t resolve your complaint, you can file an Internet Gaming Dispute Form with the DGE. That process is built around DGE-licensed operators. In a September 2023 warning about illegal sportsbooks and fantasy sports sites, the New Jersey Attorney General’s office and the DGE said such unregulated sites, which may be operated offshore, may lack dispute resolution processes.

State-required player limits and self-exclusion

New Jersey’s internet gaming rules (N.J.A.C. 13:69O-1.3 and 1.4) require licensed gaming systems to let players set daily, weekly and monthly deposit limits, daily, weekly and monthly spend limits, and a daily time limit. They also require operators to verify, when an account is opened, that the player is at least 21 and not self-excluded. The DGE runs a Self-Exclusion Program that includes an internet-gaming-only option. An offshore operator sets its own policies instead: Bovada’s site links its own Responsible Gaming and Self Exclusion pages, which are not the New Jersey program.

Payments, payouts and your data

In the same warning about illegal sportsbooks and fantasy sports sites, the Attorney General’s office and the DGE listed things those sites may lack: operational and financial controls, proper and timely payment processing, transparent ownership and anti-money laundering controls. Warning signs they named include accepting deposits or making payments in cryptocurrency, charging fees for deposits or withdrawals, bonuses that seem too good to be true, and a web domain from outside the United States. Speaking about illegal sportsbooks, DGE Director David Rebuck said: “They lack oversight to ensure customers are actually paid what they won as well as security protocols to safeguard sensitive personal and financial information, like Social Security numbers and bank data.”

New Jersey law on unlicensed operators

A 2025 New Jersey law (P.L.2025, c.128) made it a third-degree crime to operate an “online gambling resort” that lets people in New Jersey wager unless that gambling is under the regulation and control of the State. That provision is aimed at operators.
