What the 2025 New Jersey law says

The law is P.L.2025, chapter 128, which started as Assembly Bill 5447. It was approved on August 15, 2025 and took effect immediately. In plain English:

  • What counts as a sweepstakes: promotional games, online or in person, that award a prize directly or “indirectly through means such as a dual currency system of payment” that lets players exchange that currency for a prize.
  • What is banned: offering or conducting a sweepstakes that a person present in New Jersey can join by paying something of value, such as an entry fee, for a chance at a prize. The law calls this unlawful gambling under the New Jersey Constitution.
  • Who it targets: the operator or sponsor, plus its officers, employees and agents. They face civil liability and, where it applies, criminal liability.
  • Penalties: a civil penalty of up to $100,000 for a first offense and up to $250,000 for each later offense. Each day a violation continues counts as a separate offense.
  • Enforcement: the Division of Consumer Affairs enforces the sweepstakes ban and its penalties. The same law also gives the Division of Gaming Enforcement new powers against other unlawful gambling, but those sections expressly exclude sweepstakes.
  • What is still allowed: ordinary promotional sweepstakes that meet the law’s conditions. There must be a free way to enter. Any paid entry has to come with buying food, non-alcoholic drinks or merchandise worth no more than $20 (or another amount the Division of Consumer Affairs sets). That merchandise can’t be coins, tokens or online credits that are only good for entries or can be exchanged for money.

The same law also made it a third-degree crime to operate an “online gambling resort” that lets people in New Jersey wager, unless that gambling is under the regulation and control of the State.
